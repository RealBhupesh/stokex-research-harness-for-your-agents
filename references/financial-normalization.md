# Financial normalization

Reconcile reported history before forecasting. Keep reported and normalized values side by side.

For each adjustment record the line item, period, reported value, normalized value, direction, source IDs, rationale, recurrence classification and uncertainty. Check exceptional income and expenses, capitalized costs, acquisitions and disposals, subsidies, foreign exchange, stock compensation, dilution, leases, working-capital reversals, receivable quality, contingent liabilities, guarantees and related parties.

Reconcile income statement, balance sheet and cash flow. Explain differences between profit, operating cash flow and free cash flow. Inspect notes and auditor language. Do not normalize away recurring bad outcomes.

Use sector-specific models. Banks and NBFCs require credit growth, spreads, asset quality, provisions, capital and liability franchise. Insurers require premium, persistency, claims, solvency and embedded-value economics. Commodity and cyclical companies require mid-cycle volumes, prices, costs and capital intensity.

The gate fails when units, periods, share counts, cash balances, debt, material adjustments or statement linkages cannot be reconciled.
