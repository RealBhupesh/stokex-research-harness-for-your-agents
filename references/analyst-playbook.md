# Analyst playbook: what to check before qualifying a stock

This is the ordered checklist a buy-side analyst covering Indian equities works through before a stock becomes a `RESEARCH CANDIDATE`. Thresholds are configurable starting points, not validated cutoffs. Record each check in the [evidence packet](../templates/evidence-packet.md) with source ID and date. Use the sector overlays in [sector KPIs](sector-kpis.md) and [sectors](sectors.md), and the red-flag rules in [forensics](forensics-and-traps.md) and [management credibility](management-credibility.md).

Primary-source shorthand used below:

- **AR**: annual report, including the notes, auditor's report, CARO annexure, related-party note, contingent liabilities and segment note.
- **Results**: quarterly results filed under SEBI LODR Reg 33 on NSE/BSE, plus the investor presentation.
- **Con-call**: the earnings-call transcript or audio the company files on NSE/BSE, usually within a few days of results.
- **SHP**: quarterly shareholding pattern (LODR Reg 31) on NSE/BSE, including the promoter encumbrance table.
- **Reg 30**: material event disclosures under LODR Reg 30, such as orders, acquisitions, litigation, rating actions, KMP or auditor changes and fund raises.
- **PIT/SAST**: insider-trading disclosures under SEBI PIT Reg 7, and substantial-acquisition and encumbrance disclosures under SAST Reg 29 and Reg 31.
- **MF**: monthly scheme portfolios on AMC websites via AMFI links.
- **Rating**: rating rationales from CRISIL, ICRA, CARE, India Ratings and Acuité.

## 1. Business model and moat

- **Look at:** revenue by segment, geography, customer and product. Ask who pays, why, how often, and what it costs the customer to switch. Classify any moat as scale cost advantage, network, brand, regulatory licence, switching cost, distribution or process know-how. Name it or say none.
- **Where:** AR segment note and MD&A, the DRHP/RHP if the company listed recently (the best single business description available), investor presentation.
- **Green:** gross margin stable or rising through an input-cost cycle; top-10 customer share falling; pricing taken without volume loss; one moat source you can defend with numbers.
- **Disqualify:** you cannot explain in two sentences how the company makes money; one customer, one product or one licence is more than about 40% of profit with no contractual protection; the "moat" is a promoter relationship with the government.

## 2. Industry structure and pricing power

- **Look at:** market share trend of the top 3–5 players, capacity additions announced by all players versus demand growth, import competition, anti-dumping or customs protection and when it expires, regulation of pricing (DPCO for pharma, tariff orders for utilities, MRP for FMCG).
- **Where:** peer ARs and presentations, industry body data (SIAM, CMA for cement, ISMA for sugar, TRAI for telecom, IBEF only as a lead), DGTR notifications, CBIC customs notifications.
- **Green:** consolidating industry; leader gaining share while holding price; utilisation rising toward the level that historically triggered price hikes.
- **Disqualify:** a capacity wave (for example industry additions of more than about 1.5x expected demand growth over 2–3 years) with no pricing discipline; protection that lapses inside the thesis horizon and is priced as permanent.

## 3. Earnings engine

- **Look at:** decompose revenue into volume × price/mix (× FX for exporters). Split margins into gross margin (input costs, mix), employee cost, other expenses, and the fixed-cost share. Estimate operating leverage: the change in EBIT % for each 1% of revenue change, from the last two cycles.
- **Estimate upgrades usually come from:** volume ahead of plan, price hikes sticking, falling input costs not yet passed through, new capacity ramping faster, mix shift to higher margin, lower interest cost after deleveraging, or a lower tax rate.
- **Estimate downgrades usually come from:** channel destocking, delayed capacity commissioning, working-capital build that forces borrowing, rising input costs that cannot be passed on, a client ramp-down, or a regulatory price cut.
- **Where:** results, con-call (management's volume and margin guidance), monthly data where it exists (auto volumes, cement dispatch proxies, SIAM, GST, RBI sectoral credit), and consensus revisions if a licensed source exists.
- **Green:** you can name the two or three variables that explain most of the EPS change, and monthly data already points to the next quarter.
- **Disqualify:** EPS growth depends on other income, a tax write-back or capitalised costs rather than operations.

## 4. Earnings quality

- **Check** (starting-point flags, sector-adjusted):
  - CFO/PAT under about 0.7 on a 3–5 year cumulative basis.
  - Receivable, inventory or payable days rising for 2+ years while revenue grows.
  - Other income above about 20% of PBT.
  - CWIP or intangibles growing faster than revenue, which can signal capitalised expenses.
  - Contingent liabilities above about 20% of net worth.
  - Frequent "exceptional" items, changes in depreciation policy, or a big gap between standalone and consolidated profit that is not explained.
  - Rising unbilled revenue or contract assets.
  - Factoring or supplier finance used to make CFO look better. Read the AR note on trade receivables and the borrowings footnotes.
- **Where:** AR cash-flow statement and notes, auditor's report (Key Audit Matters, Emphasis of Matter, qualifications), CARO remarks, and [financial normalization](financial-normalization.md).
- **Disqualify:** a qualified audit opinion, disclaimer or going-concern paragraph; auditor resignation citing information not provided; cumulative CFO negative while PAT is positive over 3+ years for a non-lender.

## 5. Balance sheet and capital allocation

- **Look at:**
  - ROCE (pre-tax EBIT / capital employed) against a WACC assumption. India large caps are often modelled at about 11–13% in INR. That is an assumption, so state it.
  - Incremental ROCE: change in EBIT divided by change in capital employed over 3 years. It tells you more than the ROCE level.
  - Capex cycle stage: announced, under construction, commissioning, ramp-up. Check funding mix (internal accruals, debt, equity).
  - Net debt/EBITDA, interest cover, and debt maturity against CFO.
  - Dilution history (QIP, preferential allotments, warrants, ESOP pool) and acquisition track record.
- **Where:** AR, results, Reg 30 fund-raise and capex announcements, rating rationales (liquidity section).
- **Green:** incremental ROCE above WACC; capex funded mostly from accruals; dividends and buybacks sized to FCF; no serial equity issues.
- **Disqualify:** ROCE below WACC for 5+ years with no credible turnaround; repeated preferential allotments or warrants to promoter entities at prices near the SEBI floor before good news; debt raised to fund dividends or promoter-linked loans.

## 6. Management quality and track record

- **Look at:**
  - Guidance against delivery for the last 8–12 quarters: build the table from con-call transcripts (see [management credibility](management-credibility.md)).
  - Whether the explanation of misses is consistent over time or changes every quarter.
  - Related-party transactions as a % of revenue and net worth; loans and ICDs to group companies.
  - Auditor changes and their stated reasons.
  - CFO, CS and other KMP exits (Reg 30 filings).
  - Promoter remuneration against PAT (flag above about 5–10% of PAT for mid-caps, and look for commission structures).
  - Board independence and independent-director resignations.
- **Where:** con-call transcripts, AR Corporate Governance Report, RPT note, Reg 30 disclosures, postal-ballot results (institutional votes against RPT resolutions are informative), proxy-advisor reports (IiAS, InGovern, SES) as leads.
- **Green:** conservative guidance that is usually met or beaten; bad news disclosed early; capital allocation explained in numbers.
- **Disqualify:** two or more of these in 24 months: an auditor exit mid-term, a CFO exit, an independent-director resignation citing governance, RPTs growing faster than revenue, restated financials.

## 7. Promoter behaviour

- **Look at:**
  - Open-market purchases or sales by promoters and the promoter group (PIT Reg 7(2), SAST Reg 29).
  - Creeping acquisition by promoters holding 25% or more. The SAST annual limit applies; verify the current percentage and the cap.
  - Inter-se transfers. These are usually neutral; classify them before you interpret anything.
  - Pledge and encumbrance level and trend, including non-disposal undertakings (SHP encumbrance table, SAST Reg 31 filings).
  - Preferential warrants to promoters, and offer-for-sale (OFS) by promoters.
- **Where:** NSE/BSE insider-trading and SAST pages, SHP, and [ownership](ownership.md).
- **Green:** repeated open-market buying at market prices; pledges falling; promoter warrants converted at a premium.
- **Disqualify:** pledged share above about 50% of promoter holding and rising, especially with a falling price (margin-call spiral risk); promoters selling into company "good news"; promoter stake falling steadily with no disclosed reason.

## 8. Institutional positioning

- **Look at:**
  - FII/FPI, DII and MF stake trend over 4–8 quarters from the SHP.
  - The number of MF schemes holding the stock and month-on-month changes in shares held, from monthly MF portfolios.
  - Named holders above 1% in the SHP.
  - Sell-side coverage count and how it is changing. Rising coverage in a mid-cap often comes before rerating; you may need a licensed source for this.
  - Index membership and upcoming eligibility (see [short-term playbook](short-term-playbook.md) on rebalances).
  - Free float, and whether FPI headroom is near its sectoral cap (NSDL FPI monitoring).
- **Green:** under-owned by institutions with rising MF scheme count; new marquee long-only holders; coverage initiations.
- **Red:** crowded (FII+DII high and flat) with decelerating earnings, which means sellers are waiting; a stake held by a single fund that could be forced to sell; FPI headroom near the limit.

## 9. Valuation against its own history, peers and growth

- **Look at:**
  - P/E on forward and trailing earnings against its own 5–10 year band and peer median.
  - PEG, only when growth is durable.
  - EV/EBITDA for capital-heavy or leveraged firms; P/B against ROE for lenders (justified P/B ≈ (ROE − g)/(COE − g)).
  - FCF yield for mature firms; SOTP for groups.
  - Reverse DCF: what growth the price implies ([expectations](expectations.md)).
- **Rerating triggers:** ROCE step-up, a debt-free milestone, mix shift to annuity revenue, index inclusion, governance clean-up, demerger, promoter change, entry into a sector the market pays for (defence, electronics manufacturing, CDMO).
- **Green:** the price implies growth below what the earnings engine (item 3) supports, and a trigger is identifiable.
- **Disqualify for new money:** the implied growth needs peak margins and peak volumes held for a decade; the multiple sits at the top of the band while earnings revisions are turning down.

## 10. Variant view and consensus gap

- **Look at:** the consensus number (a licensed source, or broker reports the company has disclosed), your driver-based forecast, and the specific variable where you differ. Say why the market is wrong: it extrapolates the last quarter, misreads a one-off, ignores a segment, or is anchored to an old cycle.
- **Where:** [variant perception](variant-perception.md) and the [expectations map](../templates/expectations-variant-map.md).
- **Disqualify:** no quantified difference from consensus. Then you have a watch-list name, not a thesis.

## 11. Catalysts and timeline

- **List** dated events that force the market to update: results, capacity commissioning, approvals (USFDA, DCGI, PLI disbursal), order finalisation, rating upgrade, index inclusion, demerger record date, lock-in expiry (which is negative supply), promoter-stake changes. Use [catalyst underwriting](catalyst-underwriting.md) and the [market calendar](india-market-calendar.md).
- **Disqualify for a time-bound mandate:** no catalyst inside the horizon, or the key catalyst is binary and cannot be sized.

## 12. Pre-mortem and kill criteria

- **Write** "It is 12 months later and this lost 30%. Why?" Give the three most likely causes and one early indicator for each: a monthly data point, a con-call metric, a filing type. Turn them into kill criteria that can be measured and have a date. For example: "two consecutive quarters of CFO/PAT < 0.5", "promoter pledge rises above X%", "order inflow below ₹Y crore by Q3 con-call".
- **Where:** the [risk register](../templates/risk-register.md) and [thesis monitor](../templates/thesis-monitor.md).

## Qualify / watch / reject grid

| Area | Qualify | Watch | Reject |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business and moat | Moat named and evidenced | Moat plausible, unproven | Cannot explain economics |
| Industry | Consolidating or leader gaining | Neutral | Capacity glut, protection expiring |
| Earnings engine | Drivers identified, monthly data supportive | Drivers identified, data mixed | EPS from other income or one-offs |
| Earnings quality | CFO/PAT ≥ ~0.8, stable WC days | 0.6–0.8 or WC days drifting | Audit qualification; CFO persistently negative |
| Capital allocation | Incremental ROCE > WACC, low dilution | ROCE ≈ WACC, capex in progress | Serial dilution, RPT leakage |
| Management | Guidance met, clean governance | One unexplained miss or exit | Auditor/CFO/ID exit cluster |
| Promoter | Buying or stable, pledges falling | Stable, modest pledge | High and rising pledge, selling into news |
| Positioning | Under-owned, rising MF count | Neutral | Crowded with decelerating earnings |
| Valuation | Implied growth < supportable growth | Fair | Needs peak everything forever |
| Variant | Quantified gap with evidence | Qualitative only | None |
| Catalyst | Dated, inside horizon | Undated or outside horizon | None, or unsizable binary |
| Kill criteria | Measurable and dated | Vague | Not written |

Any Reject cell in earnings quality, management or promoter is a hard `REJECT` whatever the other scores. Qualify needs every cell at Qualify or Watch, with no more than three at Watch. Then route to the [investment committee](../skills/india-equity-investment-committee/SKILL.md).

## How great analysts differ from average ones

- **Track incremental change, not levels.** The market prices the second derivative: margin direction, change in growth, change in working-capital days. A 25% ROCE that is falling usually loses to a 14% ROCE that is rising.
- **Read the notes before the presentation.** Contingent liabilities, RPTs, the auditor's KAMs, and the gap between standalone and consolidated profit show up in the AR before they reach the headline.
- **Triangulate.** Management claims are checked against independent monthly data: auto volumes, GST, RBI sectoral credit, port cargo, power demand, cement dispatch proxies, pharma IQVIA-type data where licensed, and peer commentary in the same week. One peer's con-call often reveals another's quarter.
- **Model the three variables that matter.** Skip the 40-line spreadsheet with no view. Write sensitivities for the drivers that move EPS most.
- **Know what is priced in.** A great company at a price implying 30% growth for 10 years is a poor stock. Reverse-engineer the price first.
- **Update on evidence, quickly and asymmetrically.** Cut when kill criteria trigger. Don't rewrite the thesis to fit the price. Keep the original prediction frozen ([prediction record](../templates/prediction-record.md)).
- **Size by conviction and by liquidity.** Position size is capped by what can be exited in a few days at a small share of ADV. A high-conviction illiquid small cap still gets a small position.
- **Separate the business call from the timing call.** A correct thesis with no catalyst can tie up capital for years. State both.
- **Keep a calibration record.** Log hit rates by setup, sector and horizon ([analyst performance lab](analyst-performance-lab.md)). Don't claim an edge you have not measured.
