# Project Saffron: Investment Committee Memorandum

_Saffron Consumer Brands Ltd | Branded packaged foods and spices | Proposed take-private by a financial sponsor_

> **Illustrative case. The company and every figure are fictional and exist to show the method. Nothing here is investment, legal or tax advice.**

**Recommendation: APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS.** Bid ₹475.00 per share (25% premium to the unaffected ₹380.00), with a walk-away price of ₹478.33.

## 1. Executive summary

- Entry at ₹5,640 cr enterprise value, 13.8x LTM EBITDA of ₹408 cr; equity purchase ₹5,700 cr.
- Financing: ₹1,632 cr of debt (4.00x LTM EBITDA) and ₹3,655 cr of sponsor equity, plus ₹570 cr promoter rollover; equity is 72% of the capitalisation.
- Base case: 20.2% IRR and 2.51x MOIC over 5 years, exiting in FY31 at 13.5x EBITDA of ₹861 cr.
- Probability-weighted across cases: 17.3% IRR and 2.38x MOIC.
- Monte Carlo (2,000 paths): median IRR 19.7%, 5th-95th percentile 12.5% to 26.1%; 46% clear the hurdle, 0.0% lose money and 10% breach a covenant.
- Downside case: 1.05x MOIC (1.0% IRR); capital is preserved.
- Maximum bid ₹478.33, set by base-case hurdle IRR.

### Conditions

- Reset or re-sculpt covenants: the downside case breaches DSCR in FY27.
- Hold a walk-away price of ₹478.33: headroom to the maximum bid is only 0.7%.
- Only 46% of simulated outcomes clear the hurdle; underwrite the value-creation plan before signing.
- The standalone DCF (₹431.22) is below the offer: returns rely on the sponsor plan and the exit multiple, not on buying below intrinsic value.

## 2. Investment thesis and what has to be true

- Regional leader in spices and ready-to-cook mixes with 38% share in its core states and under-penetrated modern trade and e-commerce.
- Founder-led family business facing a succession decision; the promoter group is a willing seller and will roll 10% of the equity.
- Value creation from distribution expansion into two adjacent states, premiumisation of the mix and procurement discipline on commodity spices.

The base case needs four things to happen:

- Revenue compounds at 12.4% a year from ₹2,400 cr to ₹4,305 cr.
- The EBITDA margin expands from 17.0% to 20.0%.
- Free cash flow pays down ₹1,153 cr of net debt.
- A buyer pays 13.5x EBITDA in FY31, against 13.8x at entry.

## 8. India structuring considerations

These are the points deal counsel and tax advisers need to confirm for this structure. Rules change; check the current position before relying on any of them.

- Takeover Code: buying the promoter stake pushes the acquirer past 25%, which triggers a mandatory open offer for at least 26% more under the SEBI SAST Regulations. The offer price has to clear the regulation's floor (including the volume-weighted average market price tests), so the floor may sit above our model price.
- Delisting: taking the company private needs 90% ownership after the offer under the SEBI Delisting Regulations, with price discovery by reverse book building (or the newer fixed-price route where it is available). Model the outcome where delisting fails: the sponsor stays a listed majority owner and the remaining public shareholders keep their claim on dividends.
- Acquisition debt: Indian banks have historically been restricted from funding share acquisitions, and ECB end-use rules bar foreign currency borrowing for buying shares. That is why the acquisition debt here is HoldCo NCDs placed with private credit funds (FPIs or AIFs). RBI has been loosening acquisition-finance rules, so check whether bank debt is now cheaper.
- Structural subordination: HoldCo NCD holders rank behind OpCo creditors and are paid from dividends the OpCo upstreams. Section 67 of the Companies Act restricts a public company from giving financial assistance to buy its own shares, so OpCo security and debt push-down normally wait until the company is delisted and converted to private, or merged with the HoldCo.
- Interest deductibility: the model applies a 30%-of-EBITDA cap (s.94B) to the HoldCo NCDs. That disallows ₹81 cr of interest over the plan; the disallowed amount is carried forward and deducted later as headroom allows. The cap applies only to debt from, or guaranteed by, non-resident associated enterprises. If the NCDs are placed without a sponsor guarantee it may not apply. Check withholding tax on interest paid to FPIs as well.
- Tax rate: 25.17% under the concessional regime (s.115BAA), with no MAT. The Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act from April 2026 and renumbers these provisions; confirm the equivalent sections.

## 9. Key risks and mitigants

| Risk | Evidence from the model | Mitigant / diligence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin expansion does not happen | Plan takes EBITDA margin from 17.0% to 20.0%; 9.0% lower EBITDA trips a covenant. | Procurement and pricing diligence on the commodity spice basket; management incentive tied to EBITDA. |
| Exit multiple compresses | Each 1.0x of exit multiple moves IRR by about 1.8 percentage points; 1.0x is returned at 5.4x. | Exit planning for a strategic sale or IPO; a credible dual-track route. |
| Leverage too heavy in the early years | Net leverage 3.75x at close and 3.06x after year one; minimum EBITDA cushion 7.4% in the base case. | Covenant set with at least 25% headroom; equity cure rights; revolver sized for working-capital peaks. |
| Working capital absorbs cash | The downside case adds inventory days; cash sweep stops while the revolver is drawn. | Inventory and channel-stock diligence; seasonal working-capital facility. |
| Regulatory path (open offer, delisting) | Returns assume 100% ownership and full cash access. | Model the outcome where delisting fails; secure promoter support and irrevocable undertakings. |

## 10. What would make us wrong

- If revenue grows 0.2 percentage points a year slower than plan, the base case lands exactly at the 20% hurdle.
- If buyers in FY31 pay less than 13.4x, we miss the hurdle.
- If the standalone DCF (₹431.22) is right about intrinsic value, the offer pays ₹43.78 a share above it. That premium is paid for the sponsor plan, so the plan has to be delivered.
- If a strategic bidder emerges at precedent multiples, we should not chase: our ability to pay stops at ₹478.33.

## 11. Diligence priorities and first 100 days

1. Commercial diligence on the adjacent-state expansion: distributor economics, shelf share and the pace of the revenue plan.
2. Gross margin bridge: commodity hedging, pass-through lag and premium-mix assumptions.
3. Quality of earnings: normalise promoter-related transactions and one-offs; reconcile cash conversion.
4. Working capital: inventory ageing, channel stock and receivable quality by channel.
5. Tax and structure: s.94B exposure, withholding on NCD interest, stamp duty and the post-delisting merger plan.
6. 100-day plan: CFO and procurement hires, monthly KPI pack (fill rate, distribution reach, gross margin by SKU) and a lender reporting calendar.

## Appendix: model integrity and method

- Integrity checks: all pass. Sources equal uses, the balance sheet balances and cash ties in every year, and the value-creation bridge reconciles, each to within 0.000001.
- The Excel model uses live formulas throughout. The STOCKEX tests recompute every formula from the inputs and require it to match this engine, and LibreOffice recalculation is checked the same way when it is installed.
- Conventions: fiscal years end in March; the deal closes on the last balance sheet date; interest accrues on opening balances; goodwill is the price over book equity, with no purchase-price allocation write-ups; deferred tax is ignored.
- Monte Carlo paths are seeded and reproducible. Sensitivities and breakevens re-run the full integrated model.
