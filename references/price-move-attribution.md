# Price-move attribution

Answer "why is it rising or falling?" with competing hypotheses and confidence labels, not a nearby-headline story.

1. Define exact start and end timestamps and adjust for dividends, splits, bonuses, rights and other actions.
2. Compare total return with Nifty or another stated broad benchmark, a sector index and an explicit peer basket.
3. Inspect volume, delivery, spread, gaps, circuits, volatility and liquidity using dated observations.
4. Build a timestamped event ledger from exchange filings, company disclosures, official releases and credible reporting.
5. Test market, sector, factor, flow, technical and company-specific explanations.
6. Use peers, suppliers or customers as counterfactuals where valid.
7. State whether earnings expectations, discount rates, risk perception or share supply and demand changed.

Label proposed causes:

- `CONFIRMED`: a primary disclosure and market timing directly support the mechanism.
- `STRONGLY SUPPORTED`: multiple independent facts and comparisons support it, but causality is not proven.
- `PLAUSIBLE`: timing and mechanism fit, but evidence is incomplete or alternatives remain.
- `UNEXPLAINED`: no defensible explanation closes the residual.

Always preserve unexplained residuals. A sector-wide move should not be credited to company news without incremental relative performance. A price move is evidence about expectations and positioning, not proof that a thesis is correct.
