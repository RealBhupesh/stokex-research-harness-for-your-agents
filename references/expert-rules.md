# Senior analyst decision rules
These are analytical practices, not credentials or claims of access to experts.

1. Separate a good business from a good investment at this price. Describe the expectation already embedded in the quote.
2. Write the strongest bear case before concluding. Identify a measurable fact that would change the view.
3. Read footnotes: audit qualifications, related-party transactions, contingencies, revenue recognition, capitalized costs, guarantees, dilution and management remuneration.
4. Reconcile profit with operating cash flow across a cycle. Inspect receivables, inventory, payables and factoring. Growing sales with deteriorating collection quality can invalidate the growth story.
5. Judge incremental returns and reinvestment runway, not ROE in isolation. Leverage, buybacks and tiny equity bases can inflate ROE.
6. Normalize cycle peaks, one-off income and acquisition effects. Do not subtract real recurring costs merely because management calls them exceptional.
7. Link catalysts to cash flows, earnings, risk or the valuation multiple. “Sector will grow” does not establish shareholder value.
8. Use differentiated evidence to explain why the market might be wrong. Never claim a view is non-consensus without an actual expectation reference.
9. Compare against peers and a relevant diversified benchmark alternative. A stock must justify its concentration and research burden.
10. Never let low P/E override governance or solvency concerns. Never let a high score compensate for an untradeable exit.
11. Record “what must be true” in each scenario, including financing, dilution and terminal assumptions. Show sensitivity rather than a precise-looking target with weak inputs.
12. For trading, define invalidation and time stop before entry. Do not widen a stop to preserve the story, average down by default or relabel losses as long term investments.
13. Size to portfolio exposure, liquidity and stress loss as well as planned stop risk. Multiple correlated positions can fail together.
14. Treat social media, delivery percentage, block trades and technical indicators as leads or context. They do not reveal intent or prove accumulation.
15. Write down original inputs and predictions. Review process quality as well as returns, distinguish luck from repeatable evidence.

Useful investigation prompts: Which metric improves only because the denominator shrank? What is absent from the presentation but present in the filing? How much of enterprise value depends on the terminal period? What happens if the catalyst is delayed by six months? What evidence supports cash conversion after rapid growth? Who bears losses if financing tightens? What would make an index alternative preferable?
