# News, announcements and market narrative

The goal is to identify information that can change cash flows, risk, timing, financing or valuation. A large quantity of headlines is not stronger evidence.

## Retrieval sequence

1. Search the issuer's NSE/BSE announcements and company IR for the research window, starting with the latest filing and any correction or withdrawal.
2. Search SEBI, RBI, CCI, courts, ministries or sector regulators when they own the event.
3. Use high-quality financial news to add independent context, counterparties and market reaction. Obtain a second independent source for material disputed claims.
4. Use interviews and social media only as attributed claims or discovery. A rumour remains a rumour until confirmed. Look for the company's exchange clarification.
5. Search both company and key subsidiary names, old names, promoters, product brands, material geographies and sector drivers.

Record event occurrence date separately from publication date and retrieval time. Search results must be current for the requested horizon. Detect syndicated duplicates and trace them to the earliest primary source. Do not count the same press release ten times.

## Materiality model

Classify each item by mechanism:

- earnings or guidance;
- orders, contracts, capacity, pricing or product launch;
- financing, rating, covenant, default or capital raise;
- acquisition, sale, demerger, buyback or corporate action;
- management, auditor, governance, related-party or ownership change;
- litigation, tax, regulatory, environmental or licence action;
- macro, rate, currency, commodity, policy or geopolitical exposure;
- operational incident, cybersecurity, labour, supply-chain or customer issue.

For every material item, write: verified fact, source authority, affected metric, likely direction, magnitude range or unknown, timing, base-case inclusion, what the market may have expected, price reaction over a defined window, benchmark/sector-relative reaction where data exists, alternative explanation, confirmation condition and thesis impact. Do not call a reaction abnormal without an explicit event-study model.

Use qualitative grades instead of fake precision:

- Materiality: BLOCKER, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW.
- Status: CONFIRMED, COMPANY CLAIM, REGULATORY PROCESS, DISPUTED, RUMOUR.
- Novelty: NEW, PARTLY PRICED/KNOWN, REPEAT/STALE, UNKNOWN.
- Horizon: IMMEDIATE, NEXT RESULTS, 6–24 MONTHS, STRUCTURAL.

A headline is HIGH only if a plausible mechanism can materially change forecast cash flow, capital, solvency, governance, catalyst timing or the valuation multiple. Price movement alone is not proof of fundamental materiality.

## Catalyst freshness

Record the first public availability time, whether the event was previously guided, subsequent confirmation, percentage of annual revenue/order book/market capitalization where meaningful, execution period, and price/estimate change since disclosure. Mark it NEW, ACTIVE, SUBSTANTIALLY PRICED/KNOWN, SUPERSEDED or STALE. Do not apply an arbitrary exponential half-life as if every order, court case and earnings surprise decays alike. A numerical decay function may be used only after event-type-specific out-of-sample validation.

Summarize net thesis impact only after reviewing both positive and negative evidence. State what is already included in estimates or valuation. For a trade, list events inside the intended holding window. For long term, distinguish temporary noise from permanent economics. No automated monitoring exists unless separately configured.
