# Variant perception

A stock is not attractive merely because the business is good. Identify the material difference between what the price appears to require and what the evidence supports.

Separate:

1. observable facts;
2. published consensus or clearly labeled reverse-valuation expectations;
3. the independent driver forecast;
4. the variant view.

Each variant states the disputed metric, market-implied range, independent range, mechanism, magnitude, horizon, source IDs, disconfirming evidence, confidence, catalyst and what is already priced in. Do not invent consensus. If no defensible variant exists, use `WATCH`, `REJECT` or `INSUFFICIENT DATA`.

Test alternative explanations, base effects, cyclicality, accounting changes, acquisitions, dilution and selection bias. A famous owner or bullish management statement cannot serve as the variant.
