# Expectations implied by price

Good companies can be poor investments when the quote embeds stronger outcomes than the evidence supports. Run an expectations check for valuation-dependent recommendations.

## Reverse valuation workflow

1. Reconcile current enterprise value or equity value, diluted shares and non-operating claims.
2. Select the model appropriate to the sector. Do not use industrial FCFF logic for banks.
3. Hold a transparent set of assumptions constant and solve for one unknown at a time: explicit growth, terminal margin, sustainable ROE, reinvestment return, terminal growth or exit multiple.
4. Compare the implied assumption with the company's history, credible capacity, industry structure, management guidance, relevant peer forecasts and downside cases.
5. Vary discount rate, terminal assumptions and normalization. If a small change produces a different conclusion, label the result fragile.

## Expectations gap

`expectations gap = evidence-backed scenario assumption - price-implied assumption`.

Do not combine unlike units. Show a vector of gaps, not one magic score. A positive growth gap can be offset by worse cash conversion, dilution or required capital. “Consensus” must have a dated source, coverage and definition; otherwise call it management guidance, market-implied, or the harness estimate.

The helper can solve an explicit FCFF growth rate under a simplified model. It is a diagnostic, not a full forecast. Always show the current price/EV, equation, solved variable, bracket, other fixed assumptions and failure cases.
