# Notable-holder and insider analysis

Ownership can reveal alignment, concentration, changing conviction or market attention. It is not borrowed credibility and does not prove a good future return. Analyze what was disclosed, by whom, for which date and under what reporting rule.

## Source hierarchy

1. NSE/BSE shareholding pattern and exchange-filed disclosures.
2. Insider/PIT, SAST, promoter encumbrance and acquisition disclosures.
3. Exchange bulk and block-deal records, which show a transaction but not necessarily the buyer's complete or continuing position.
4. Fund-house and AMFI-linked monthly portfolio disclosures for mutual funds.
5. Investor or fund letters and interviews, only for stated rationale, never as proof of current holdings unless reconciled to a dated disclosure.
6. Reputable news reports as discovery leads. Social-media posts, screenshots and portfolio websites are unverified until matched to primary evidence.

## Identity rules

- Record legal holder name exactly. Do not convert a fund, LLP, trust, relative, PMS, AIF or company into a famous individual's personal holding without evidence of beneficial ownership and control.
- Match issuer, ISIN, share class and quarter-end date. Show shares, percentage, filing date, transaction date if relevant, source ID and direct versus linked ownership.
- Compare at least the available recent quarters on the same basis. `Not listed by name` can mean the holding is below a disclosure threshold, aggregated in a category, held through another vehicle or absent. It does not prove a complete exit.
- Distinguish promoter, promoter group, director/KMP, institutional, mutual fund, FPI, government and named public shareholder. Category movements can reflect reclassification.
- Separate holdings from flow. A bulk purchase is not a quarter-end holding; a quarter-end holding does not reveal the acquisition price or current intent.
- Corporate actions change share counts. Compare percentages and split-adjusted shares.
- Insider buying can arise from an approved trading plan, ESOP exercise, inter-se transfer, rights issue or other non-open-market event. Classify transaction type before interpretation.

## Analytical output

Create an ownership ledger:

| Holder | Legal vehicle / relationship | Source type | As-of / transaction date | Shares / stake | Change on comparable basis | Transaction type / price | Confidence | Interpretation and alternative explanation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then answer:

1. Is ownership aligned or dangerously concentrated?
2. Are promoter pledges or encumbrances changing?
3. Is the observed change economically material relative to the holder, company and traded liquidity?
4. Is there repeated open-market accumulation, or only one ambiguous event?
5. Does the holder's likely mandate and horizon match the user's objective?
6. What evidence would show the signal has become stale?

Never say “X invested, therefore buy.” Never estimate a famous person's average price from an incomplete public record. Do not infer nonpublic reasons for a trade. Attribute opinions to a dated source and separate them from the workflow's inference.
